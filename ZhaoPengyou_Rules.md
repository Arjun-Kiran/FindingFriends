## Introduction

**Looking for Friends** is a Chinese trick-taking game for 5 to 12 or more players. It is played with multiple packs and features **variable partnerships**, which are unknown at the start of play. The object is to win tricks for your team containing **Kings, Tens, and Fives**.

When sets of identical cards are held, it is possible to lead more than one card at a time, beginning a multiple trick. Despite the large player count, the game moves quickly and is widely regarded as one of the most successful trick-taking games for 6 or more people.

Looking for Friends is closely related to **100** (打百分) and **Tractor** (拖拉机), which are played with fixed partnerships. All these games — in which a player's score or "level" is represented by a card rank — are sometimes known collectively as **Shēng Jí** (升级, "raise level") games.

## Players and Cards

The game is played with two or more identical standard packs shuffled together. The number of packs scales with player count:

- **5–7 players:** 2 packs
- **8–11 players:** 3 packs
- **12+ players:** 4 packs

Sufficient red and black jokers are included so that all cards can be distributed equally, with a **kitty of 6 (or 8) cards** left over.

### Card Requirements by Player Count

| Players | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 10 | 11 | 12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Packs | 2 | 2 | 2 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 4 |
| Red Jokers | 2 | 2 | 0 | 2 | 3 | 0 | 2 | 2 |
| Black Jokers | 2 | 2 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 2 | 0 |
| Cards per Player | 20 | 17 | 14 | 19 | 17 | 15 | 14 | 17 |
| Total Cards | 108 | 108 | 104 | 158 | 159 | 156 | 160 | 210 |

### Trumps

In each hand, all cards of a particular **suit** and a particular **rank** are trumps (e.g., all hearts and all twos). Jokers are always trumps. The trump hierarchy from highest to lowest is:

1. Red jokers
2. Black jokers
3. Cards belonging to **both** the trump rank and trump suit
4. Other cards of the trump rank (all equal — first played wins ties)
5. Other cards of the trump suit (ace down to two, excluding trump rank)

Cards of non-trump suits rank normally from ace (high) down to two (low), excluding the trump rank.

> **Example:** If **eights** and **diamonds** are trumps, the trump ranking from high to low is:  
> Red joker → Black joker → ♦8 → [♠8, ♥8, ♣8 — all equal] → ♦A → ♦K → ♦Q → ♦J → ♦10 → ♦9 → ♦7 → ♦6 → ♦5 → ♦4 → ♦3 → ♦2  
> Other suits rank: A, K, Q, J, 10, 9, 7, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2

### Card Values

| Card | Points |
|---|---|
| Each King | 10 points |
| Each Ten | 10 points |
| Each Five | 5 points |
| All other cards | 0 points |

The total card points in play is **200** (2 packs), **300** (3 packs), or **400** (4 packs).

## The Deal, Scores, and Making Trumps

### Player Levels

Each player has a **level** represented by a card rank from 2 (low) to Ace (high). All players begin at **level two**. Levels increase through wins, and the game ends when a player's level advances beyond Ace.

### The Deal

The deal and play proceed **anticlockwise**. There is no designated dealer. The pack is placed face-down in the centre and each player draws one card at a time from the top without showing it. Drawing continues until everyone has their full hand, leaving 6 face-down cards as the kitty.

### Making Trumps

During the deal, a player may **expose a card from their hand** face-up on the table, provided its rank matches their current level. The suit of that card becomes the proposed trump suit.

**Overriding trumps:**

- Another player can change the trump suit by exposing a **pair of identical cards** matching their own level.
- The original trump proposer may either accept the change (take their card back) or **defend** by exposing another matching card to form a pair, forcing the challenger to withdraw.
- With 3+ packs: a pair can be overridden by a triplet, which can be defended against by a matching third card, and so on.
- **You cannot override your own trump suit.** Once you declare a trump, you may only defend it, not change it yourself.

> **Example:**  
> - Player A (level 4) exposes ♣4 → proposes clubs as trumps  
> - Player B (level 3) exposes ♠3-♠3 → A takes back ♣4, spades proposed  
> - Player C (level 6) exposes ♥6-♥6-♥6 → B defends with a third ♠3 → C withdraws  
> - **Result:** B is the trump maker with spades as trumps

### Who Starts the Deal

- **First deal:** The starter is chosen at random (each player draws a card; highest card goes first). After the drawn cards are shuffled back in, the starter shuffles the full pack and the player to the starter's left cuts it.
- **Subsequent deals:** The player who made trumps in the previous deal starts, or the next anticlockwise active player if that player is unavailable. The starter shuffles and the player to their left cuts before each deal.
- If no one makes trumps during the deal, all cards are reshuffled and redealt by the same starter.

## Discarding and Choosing Partners

### The Kitty

The trump maker takes the 6 (or 8) face-down kitty cards **without showing them** to others, then discards the same number of cards face-down in front of themselves. These discards remain hidden until the end of play — if the opposing team wins the last trick, these discards are revealed and any Kings, Tens, or Fives among them are **counted at double value** for the defenders.

### Calling Partners

The trump maker calls a number of specific cards to determine team membership:

| Players | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 10 | 11 | 12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Max trump maker's team size | 2 | 3 | 3 | 4 | 4 | 5 | 5 | 6 |
| Min defending team size | 3 | 3 | 4 | 4 | 5 | 5 | 6 | 6 |
| Cards called by trump maker | 1 | 2 | 2 | 3 | 3 | 4 | 4 | 5 |

**Rules for calling:**

- Called cards **must not be trumps**.
- Because multiple copies of each card exist, the trump maker must specify **which copy** (e.g., "first ace of clubs", "third ace of diamonds").
- The partner is whoever plays that specific copy of the card during play.
- **Partners must not reveal themselves** except through the cards they play. No one — not even the called card holders — knows the full team composition at the start.
- If two called cards are played by the same person, or a called card is played by the trump maker, the trump maker's team will be smaller than usual.

> **Example (8 players, fives and spades are trumps):**  
> The trump maker calls the *first ace of clubs*, *first king of clubs*, and *third ace of diamonds*. The first player to play each of those cards joins the trump maker's team.

## The Play

Play proceeds **anticlockwise**. The trump maker leads to the first trick; thereafter, the winner of each trick leads to the next.

When called cards are played, they are left **face-up** in front of the player who played them, making team membership visible. Valuable cards (Kings, Tens, Fives) won by the defending team are kept face-up to make point tracking easy.

Each player contributes the **same number of cards** to a trick as were led. There are four types of leads:

---

### Single Card Lead

Any single card may be led. Other players must follow suit if able. All trump cards (trump rank + trump suit + jokers) count as a single suit.

If any trumps are played, the highest trump wins. Otherwise, the highest card of the led suit wins. Ties go to the first played.

> **Example (Queens and Diamonds are trumps):**  
> If ♠Q is led, all other players must play a diamond, a queen, or a joker. If ♠7 is led, players must play a spade — but not the queen, as she is a trump. Players with no spades may trump or discard.

---

### Leading a Set of Identical Cards

Two or more **identical** cards (same rank *and* same suit) may be led together. Cards equal in rank but different in suit do not qualify.

**Following rules:**

- Each player contributes the same number of cards.
- Players must follow with cards of the led suit, and **must play matching sets if possible** (pairs if pairs were led, triples if triples were led, etc.).
- If a player cannot complete a set of the led suit, they play what they have and fill the rest with any cards.

**Winning:** The trick can only be won by the highest identical set of the led suit, or if someone voids the suit, the highest identical set of trumps played.

> **Example (Nines and Clubs are trumps, a pair of ♥J is led):**
> - Hold ♥A, ♥K, ♥10, ♥6 → play any two hearts, but cannot win (no pair)
> - Hold ♥A, ♥10, ♥10, ♥6 → **must** play the pair of tens
> - Hold ♥K, ♥K, ♥10, ♥10 → may play kings (to win) or tens (to lose intentionally)
> - Hold ♥8, ♥8, ♥8, ♥6, ♥5 → **must** play two eights
> - Hold ♠A, ♠Q, ♠Q, ♣7, ♣7 (no hearts) → may play any two cards; may trump with ♣7-♣7 to win
> - Hold ♠10, ♥K, ♣7, ♣7 (no other hearts) → cannot trump; must follow suit with the ♥K. The second card must be the spade or a trump, but either way cannot win the trick
> - Hold ♣9, ♠9, ♥9, ♦K → may play any two cards; the ♥9 is a trump, so you have no hearts. Whatever you play cannot win the trick (two nines are equal in rank but not identical)

---

### Leading a Sequence of Sets

If you hold two or more **equally-sized sets of identical cards in adjacent ranks of the same suit**, you may lead them together as a sequence. These are sometimes called **tractors**.

**Restrictions:**

- Sets must be the **same suit**, **same size**, and **adjacent in rank**.
- The trump rank and jokers **cannot** be included in a sequence.
- Adjacency skips the trump rank (e.g., if Fives are trump, Six and Four are adjacent).

> **Examples (Fives and Hearts are trumps):**
>
> **Valid leads:**
> - ♣8-♣8-♣7-♣7
> - ♦K-♦K-♦K-♦Q-♦Q-♦Q
> - ♥Q-♥Q-♥J-♥J-♥10-♥10
> - ♠6-♠6-♠4-♠4 (six and four are adjacent because fives are trump)
>
> **Invalid leads:**
> - ♣8-♣8-♠7-♠7 (different suits)
> - ♣9-♣9-♣7-♣7 (not adjacent ranks)
> - ♣9-♣9-♣9-♣8-♣8 (sets must be the same size)
> - ♥6-♥6-♥5-♥5 (not adjacent, as fives are trump, and cards of the trump rank cannot be used)
> - ♦5-♦5-♥A-♥A (cards of the trump rank cannot be used in sequences)

**Following rules:**

- Players must follow suit and play sets of the same size as in the sequence, if possible.
- Players are not required to follow with a sequence — any sets of the right size will do.
- If a player lacks sufficient sets, they play what they can of the suit.
- If a player runs out of the led suit entirely, they may play any cards with no obligation to play sets.

**Winning:** The trick can only be won by a higher sequence of the **same shape** (same size and length) in the led suit, or a qualifying sequence in trumps.

---

### Leading a Group of Top Cards

If you believe several cards or combinations in a suit are **all unbeatable** by cards of that suit, you may lead them all at once. This lead can mix single cards, sets, and sequences of sets.

**Following rules:**

- Other players must follow with the same number of cards, playing cards of the led suit and matching set sizes as far as possible.

**Winning:** If the lead is truly unbeatable, the leader wins unless a player with no cards in the led suit plays an equivalent collection of trumps matching the structure of the lead. If multiple players trump, the one with the highest trump set matching the largest combination in the lead wins.

**Penalty for a false lead:** If any player — including a partner — holds a card or combination that can beat one of the components of the lead, they **must immediately show it**. The leader must then:

- Withdraw all cards from the lead except the beatable card/combination.
- For **each card withdrawn**, 10 card points are transferred from the leader's team to the opposing team.

> **Example (Fours and Diamonds are trumps):**  
> A player leads ♠A-♠K-♠J-♠J. If another player holds a single ♠A (beating the King), the leader takes back the Ace and both Jacks, losing 30 points, and leads only the King. If someone holds ♠Q-♠Q (beating the Jacks), the Ace and King are withdrawn at a cost of 20 points.

## Scoring

At the end of play, the **defending team** counts the card points (Kings, Tens, Fives) they won face-up in front of them. If they won the **last trick**, the trump maker's discards are revealed and any Kings, Tens, or Fives there count **double** toward the defenders' total.

### Winning Thresholds (per pack)

| Defenders' Points (per pack) | Result |
|---|---|
| 60+ | Defenders win |
| 40–59 | Neither side wins |
| Under 40 | Trump makers win |

### Level Promotion

All members of the winning team are promoted by at least one level (2 → 3 → ... → 10 → J → Q → K → A). When any player advances beyond Ace, the game ends and that player wins.

### Bonus Promotions

| Condition | Promotion |
|---|---|
| Defenders score ≥ 80 pts/pack | Defenders +2 levels |
| Defenders score ≥ 100 pts/pack | Defenders +3 levels |
| Defenders score < 20 pts/pack | Trump makers +2 levels |
| Defenders score 0 | Trump makers +3 levels |
| Trump makers win with fewer than max team size | +same again per missing player |

### Full Scoring Table

The promotion amount for trump makers (T) depends on both the defenders' point total and the trump maker's team size. The columns below show promotions for each possible team size (maximum down to 1).

**2 packs** — trump maker's team size:

| Defenders' pts | Max (2) | 1 |

| 80–115 | 0 | 0 |

> Player E makes trumps and calls two cards, both of which C plays. Defenders (A, B, D, F) take only 30 points. E and C are promoted **4 levels** each: 2 for the low defender score (< 40) and 2 more because their team had one fewer player than the maximum three.

## Variations

Many variants from related Shēng Jí games (100 and Tractor) can be applied to Looking for Friends. Common variations include:

### Deal
- Some groups choose the first card drawer by cutting the deck and counting around the table. In subsequent deals the turn passes to the right rather than staying with the trump maker.

### Kitty Size
- Some groups always use one red and one black joker per deck and allow the kitty size to vary (keeping it close to 6 cards) rather than adjusting jokers to make it exactly 6.

### Active and Passive Scores
- In an older variant, players are either **active** or **passive**. Only active players may make trumps. Winners of a hand become active; losers become passive. Points won while passive convert passive to active status, with subsequent points adding to level.
  - **Defenders win** with ≥ 40 pts/pack (no middle zone).
  - Winning normally gives **1 game point**. Active winners advance their level by one per point; passive winners use the first point to restore active status (without a level increase), then gain levels for any further points.
  - **Bonus game points** can be earned: defenders get 2 points if they score ≥ 80 pts/pack, or 3 points if ≥ 100 pts/pack. Trump makers get an extra point for each player below the maximum team size. If defenders score 0 points, trump makers score **2 game points**, plus an **additional 2 per player short of the maximum team size**.
  - **If no one makes trumps:** all players become active for the new deal, but their levels remain unchanged (e.g., a player who was passive 7 becomes active 7).

**Example of active/passive scoring (6 players):**

| | A | B | C | D | E | F |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Start of game | active 2 | active 2 | active 2 | active 2 | active 2 | active 2 |
| B, D, E win 1 pt | passive 2 | active 3 | passive 2 | active 3 | active 3 | passive 2 |
| A, B, C win 1 pt | active 2 | active 4 | active 2 | passive 3 | passive 3 | passive 2 |
| C, D, F win 2 pts | passive 2 | passive 4 | active 4 | active 4 | passive 3 | active 3 |

### Making Trumps (Variant)
- Some allow any player to make trumps by exposing a card of any rank matching **any active player's** level.
- If the exposing player is not active at that level, the next anticlockwise active player at that level becomes the trump maker.

### Calling (Two-Pack Variant)
- In a two-pack game, some allow the trump maker to call "the other" copy of a card rather than specifying first or second. This signals to that player that the trump maker holds the matching copy.

### Play Variants
- **Trump tractors above Ace:** Some allow sequences in the trump suit to continue through the trump rank, black jokers, and red jokers (e.g., A-A-[trump rank]-[trump rank] is a valid tractor).
- **Four equal cards beat a tractor:** Some play that four identical cards beat a four-card tractor.
- **Forced sub-patterns:** Some require players who cannot follow a full tractor/set to follow with the best sub-pattern they can (e.g., failing a tractor, play two pairs; failing that, one pair).
- **Top card lead constraint:** Some require that a group-of-top-cards lead contain the same number of cards of each rank.

### Scoring Variants
- **Trump maker determined by prior result:** After the first deal, some determine the next trump maker by the previous result rather than by who exposed cards.
- **Levels beyond Ace:** In one variant, play continues past Ace to a "Joker" level (no trump rank or suit; jokers are the only trumps), then the player returns to level 2. Levels above Joker are written as 2+, 3+, etc. (or 2*, 3*) on the score sheet, and the game continues as long as players wish or until an agreed deadline. Since levels 10 and K are particularly challenging (the trump rank cards are also scoring cards), some groups skip levels 2–9 on the second lap, so the progression goes: Joker → 10+ → J+ → Q+ → K+ → A+ → Joker+ → 10++ → etc.
- **Penalty for false top-card lead:** Alternatives to the standard penalty include: (1) the offending team automatically loses the hand, awarding each opponent one level; or (2) a points penalty (-2 for the offender, -1 for teammates) deducted from future scores.
